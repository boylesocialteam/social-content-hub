Social Media Strategy

Doing Less, Better.

A data-led case for reducing posting volume to unlock the time, quality and reactivity our channels are missing.

Prepared by Social Media Team Data window 15 May – 15 Jul 2026 (61 days) Source Brandwatch · X & Instagram
Executive Summary

We're producing more content than anyone for very little reward.

Over 61 days, we published more than nearly every competitor across both platforms, yet ranked at or near the bottom for engagement per post. The issue is not effort or output; it is that the current volume leaves too little capacity for quality, planning and timely reaction. Reducing low-value posting is the key change that creates space to improve all three.

It's not just how much we post, but what. Brandwatch's format breakdown for the same 15 May–15 Jul window shows we're publishing a fundamentally different content mix to the brands that are winning — and leaning hardest on the formats that engage the least.

We are the only brand in the set built on image posts (~40% of our output, ~580 posts — more image posts than anyone). Everyone else runs on text. And video — the format the algorithm rewards — is barely 5% of what we do.

Image posts are the most design-heavy to produce, yet return the least. We're spending our scarcest resource — design time — on our lowest-yield format.

Why this matters for "post less"

The engagement-by-format data is stark: on X, text and video drive almost all engagement, while image posts drive very little. We over-produce the low-yield format and under-produce the high-yield ones. Cutting image volume doesn't just save time — it frees the design hours to make the video and reactive text that actually perform.

Instagram — a different formats picture (and a harder truth)

On Instagram the story flips. The winners here are built almost entirely on video — Paddy Power ~80%, Bet365 ~90%. We're more spread out — video 44% of posts, single images 37%, carousels 19% — and our carousels barely engage. Paddy Power's video pulls ~4.7M reach and ~417K engagement; ours barely registers. Our IG problem is twofold: not enough video, and the video we make doesn't yet compete on craft.

Spotlight: Gaming content

Gaming deserves its own look, because we produce so much of it. We post around 8–9 Gaming videos every week on Instagram — of our 107 IG videos in the window, roughly two-thirds are Gaming. Yet it's our single worst-performing theme: Instagram's own breakdown shows that every one of our Gaming videos ranks below all our other content for reach, viewership and engagement. We're pouring the majority of our video effort into the content that works least — and, at sometimes two Gaming posts a day, it's a major reason we out-post the average IG competitor by 2.5×.

Organically, it adds almost nothing

Across the whole window, 71 Gaming posts earned a combined 98 engagements, 19 profile visits and zero new follows. That isn't a small underperformance — it's the majority of our Instagram video production returning next to no organic value and nothing at all in growth. The audience we've built on sport simply doesn't come to us for casino content.

The evidence it could be hurting our reach

Instagram doesn't distribute posts evenly. Head of Instagram Adam Mosseri has described an "audition system": every post is first shown to a small test group, and only pushed to a wider audience if they engage. Gaming's rock-bottom engagement means it fails that audition every time — which is exactly why its average reach is just 203. Instagram's own guidance compounds the concern:

  • Mosseri states engagement rate matters more than reach, and that you should post the content "your ideal follower will engage with" — Gaming is the opposite for a sports audience.
  • He warns that pushing volume above the optimal threshold without maintaining quality "can actually hurt your algorithmic standing." Instagram suggests ~2–4 Reels/week; we post 8–9 Gaming videos/week alone.

Instagram doesn't publicly confirm a blanket account-wide penalty, so we can't state it as fact — but every mechanism they have described points the same way: high-volume, low-engagement Gaming content is at best wasted effort, and at worst is teaching the algorithm to expect little from us and limiting the reach of the content that performs.

Section 5

What We Could Do Better With More Time

The pay-off. Each of the six branches from the plan of action, made concrete — what "good" looks like when the hours freed up by posting less are reinvested.

✍️

Better Content

Reducing the daily volume gives each post a clearer purpose: entertain, inform, react or convert. Instead of producing constant image-led filler to meet a quota, the team can spend more time on stronger concepts, sharper copy, better creative and the content themes that have already proved they can earn attention.

What changes — Fewer posts, but every post has a reason to exist and a higher creative standard.
🗓️

Better Planning

The current always-on pace leaves little room to plan beyond the immediate calendar. With fewer low-value posts to turn around each day, the team can build a proper weekly content plan around fixtures, tentpole moments, campaigns and priority products — while still leaving space for the unexpected.

What changes — Move from filling a daily schedule to planning around the moments that matter most.
🎬

More Video

Video is the clearest format opportunity across both channels, but good video needs time: concepts, filming or sourcing, editing, approvals and platform-native finishing. Cutting low-return static posts and carousels creates the production capacity to make fewer, more distinctive videos rather than treating video as another item on an already overloaded checklist.

What changes — Prioritise purposeful, higher-craft video over routine static output — especially for major fixtures, ambassadors and reactive moments.
⚡

More Reactive

A flat publishing rhythm means the team is busy even when nothing significant is happening, and has less capacity when something does. Reducing baseline volume gives the team the headspace to react quickly to match moments, fan conversation, breaking stories and cultural opportunities without sacrificing planned work or rushing the creative.

What changes — Flex output around opportunity: quieter on quiet days, faster and more ambitious when the moment is worth it.
📊

Better Analysis

When production is relentless, performance review becomes an afterthought. Protected time each week would allow the team to identify what worked, what did not, and why — then use that learning to improve the next week's content rather than repeating the same output pattern.

What changes — Introduce a simple weekly review of formats, themes, creative approaches and platform performance, with clear actions fed back into the plan.
😌

Less Team Pressure

The issue is not effort; it is a workflow built around constant output. High posting volume creates continuous briefing, design, approvals, publishing and reporting pressure, leaving little room for proactive work or recovery. A more deliberate rhythm lets the team focus on quality, make better decisions and work sustainably without reducing ambition.

What changes — Replace a constant-content treadmill with a quality-first operating model that gives the team room to plan, create, react and learn.
